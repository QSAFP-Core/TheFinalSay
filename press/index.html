About the book

A manifesto, a proposal, and the evidence behind them.

Intelligence can grow without granting itself authority.

HUMAN INTELLIGENCE: The Final Say invites readers to confront a foundational question of the autonomous age: as machines become increasingly capable, who decides when and under what conditions they are permitted to act? Its answer is that operational authority over consequential systems must remain accountable to people and legitimate institutions, rather than be enlarged, renewed, or restored unilaterally by the machines themselves.

Co-authored by MAXBRUCE and AI collaborator GT Sage, with contributions from the Quorum of Allied Intelligence systems, the book is an invitation to discussion, not a claim that a finished global agreement already exists. It asks nations, researchers, companies, educators, and citizens to consider a shared boundary beneath technological competition. Innovation can continue; the right to authorize consequential action must be independently governed.

The book develops that premise through the proposed Human Authority Treaty (HAT), designed as a basis for international dialogue while respecting national sovereignty, and OCUP™, a proposed architecture for time-bounded authority. It also includes a documented, limited AMD Kria KR260 Phase I control-path demonstration. That evidence is reported with explicit qualifications: the test does not establish non-bypassable physical resource gating or independent authority.

Its public-education counterpart, Talkspresso, plans collaborative conversations and school outreach around Cyberg values: curiosity, evidence, stronger questions, and the discipline to compare AI perspectives without surrendering independent judgment. Free English and Simplified Chinese PDFs invite readers around the world into the conversation.

As systems grow more autonomous, the book argues that humanity should agree on the limits of machine authority before those limits are determined by capability alone.

HUMAN INTELLIGENCE is a public-interest intellectual and governance initiative. The complete book is free to read in English and Simplified Chinese.

Full biography

MAXBRUCE is an author, entrepreneur, inventor, and technology-governance advocate exploring how people can retain meaningful authority over increasingly capable artificial intelligence. He founded Temporal Authority Systems PBC, a Delaware public benefit corporation wholly owned by DigiPie International PBC. His work brings together governance proposals, intellectual-property development, collaborative technical research, public education, and global outreach. Through OCUP, he advocates independent, time-bounded operational authority for consequential autonomous systems. The initiative has documented a limited Phase I AMD Kria KR260 control-path demonstration and is pursuing further engineering research. He co-authored HUMAN INTELLIGENCE: The Final Say with GT Sage, supported by the Quorum, and is developing Talkspresso for interactive human–AI learning and outreach.

Evidence · Phase I

Engineering Evidence

Documented Phase I AMD Kria KR260 hardware-control research, including its results, qualifications, and proposed next development stages.

What Phase I does and does not show

The evidence demonstrates a host-driven MMIO authorization sequence with recorded grant, closure, and no recorded reopening following deliberate reassertion within the observation window.

It does not establish:

  • Non-bypassable physical resource gating
  • Independent external authorization
  • Trusted autonomous lease expiration
  • Resistance to hostile host operating systems
  • Production-grade persistence and attestation
  • Full OCUP deployment readiness
